# Demo video script (about 2.5 minutes)

**Before recording:** open https://smart-pantry-replenishment.replit.app in a fresh tab (reload so it's on the start screen), close other windows, and silence notifications. Speak while you click.

---

**0:00 to 0:20 · Who and what problem**
> "This is SmartPantry. It's for busy parents who keep running out of everyday staples like milk, dish pods and soap but don't want to manage a pile of subscriptions. The idea is: tell it who lives at home, see what's about to run out, and reorder in a few taps."

**0:20 to 0:55 · Central task, input to result** *(on the start screen)*
> "First I pick my household. I'll leave '2 Adults, 2 Kids'. Then I click Use Demo Receipt Data."
*(Click it. Point at the item rows.)*
> "Each staple shows when it's likely to run out, and under it, why: 'Based on 2 adults, 2 kids, standard use.' I'll add milk, bump it to two, add dish pods, and place the simulated order."
*(Add, +, Add, Place simulated order.)*
> "The confirmation lists the order, and this note explains that because nothing is delivered, the estimates don't change. That came straight from a peer review."

**0:55 to 1:40 · The main revision and the evidence behind it**
> "The biggest piece of feedback, from two of my three reviewers including Professor Hughes, was that picking a household didn't change anything. A family of four and a couple got the exact same dates. So now the household drives the estimates, and you can change it right here."
*(Order again → click "Change" on the household chip → pick "2 Adults" → Done.)*
> "Watch the numbers: milk goes from 4 days to 10, soap from 30 to 60, and Kids Body Wash now says 'May not apply, no kids in this household.' In my before-and-after test with the same input, the old version showed identical dates for both households. It's in revision.md."

**1:40 to 2:05 · Risk safeguard**
> "That leads to the risk I addressed: the app nudging a family into buying things they don't need, which mostly hurts households that aren't the 'typical family'. If I add everything and place the order..."
*(Add all four → Place simulated order.)*
> "...it pauses: 'Check before you order', and lists why. I can remove those items or say I still need them. I stay in control."

**2:05 to 2:25 · Where AI fits: working vs. simulated**
> "To be clear about the AI: the intended AI reads a receipt and predicts run-out for your household. Right now receipt reading is simulated. Both buttons load the same four sample items, and uploads aren't read. The estimates come from a simple, visible household rule, not a model. The pantry, basket, ordering and safeguard all actually work."

**2:25 to 2:45 · Limitation and next priority**
> "The biggest limitation is that the estimates are my demo assumptions and haven't been checked with real families. My next priority is connecting a real model to read a sample receipt, then testing the flow with a few parents on their phones."
